HOME

Pakistan, officially the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, is a country in South Asia. It is the fifth-most populous country, with a population of over 241.5 million, having the second-largest Muslim population in the world as of 2023. Islamabad is the nation's capital, while Karachi is its largest city and financial centre. Pakistan is the 33rd-largest country by area. Bounded by the Arabian Sea on the south, the Gulf of Oman on the southwest, and the Sir Creek on the southeast, it shares land borders with India to the east; Afghanistan to the west; Iran to the southwest; and China to the northeast. It shares a maritime border with Oman in the Gulf of Oman, and is separated from Tajikistan in the northwest by Afghanistan's narrow Wakhan Corridor.

HunzaValley

The Hunza Valley is a breathtaking mountainous valley in Pakistan's Gilgit-Baltistan region, situated at an elevation of 2,438 meters and renowned for its dramatic landscapes, towering peaks like Rakaposhi, and an exceptionally high literacy rate exceeding 90%. Historically an independent principality for nearly a millennium, its cultural heartbeat remains centered in the capital of Karimabad, where ancient strongholds like the 800-year-old Baltit Fort stand alongside natural wonders like the turquoise Attabad Lake and the jagged Passu Cones. Inhabited by diverse ethnic groups who predominantly practice Ismaili Shia Islam, this vibrant valley attracts global travelers, especially during the spring blossom and autumn foliage seasons, serving as a gateway to the spectacular Khunjerab Pass on the Chinese border.

BadshahiMosque

The Badshahi Mosque (or Badshahi Masjid) is a breathtaking Mughal-era imperial mosque in Lahore, Punjab, Pakistan, widely celebrated as one of the country's most iconic and majestic architectural landmarks. Commissioned by the Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb Alamgir in 1671 and completed in 1673, this monument represents the absolute zenith of imperial Mughal design, characterized by its vast red sandstone structure, stunning white marble domes, and four towering 196-foot minarets. For over 300 years, its expansive courtyard—which can hold up to 100,000 worshippers—reigned as the largest mosque courtyard in the world.

MazareQuaid

Mazar-e-Quaid, also known as the National Mausoleum, is the iconic white marble final resting place of Muhammad Ali Jinnah, the founder of Pakistan. Located in the heart of Karachi, the monument was designed by architect Yahya Merchant and is celebrated as a masterpiece of 1960s modernist architecture, featuring a distinctive cuboid structure topped with a soaring dome and surrounded by a sprawling 53-acre neo-Mughal park. Beyond the central tomb of Jinnah, the complex serves as a national sanctuary housing the graves of other prominent founding figures, including his sister Fatima Jinnah and Pakistan's first Prime Minister, Liaquat Ali Khan. Today, it stands as a revered national symbol, drawing visitors for its daily military changing-of-the-guard ceremonies and an on-site museum showcasing Jinnah's personal artifacts, historical documents, and vintage automobiles.

Quick Facts About Pakistan

⇒👥 Population & Demographics

Pakistan has a population of over 240 million people, making it the fifth most populous country in the world. It boasts a remarkably young demographic, with more than 60% of its population under the age of 30. The country is a ethnic melting pot, comprising major groups such as Punjabis, Pashtuns, Sindhis, Balochis, and Muhajirs, which contributes to a rich tapestry of local traditions, cuisines, and arts.

⇒🗣️ Language & Religion

While Urdu is the national language and serves as a unifying medium of communication, English is widely used in government, business, and higher education. The country is also home to several regional languages like Punjabi, Pashto, Sindhi, and Balochi. In terms of faith, Pakistan is predominantly Islamic, with Muslims making up about 96% of the population, alongside vibrant minority communities of Hindus, Christians, and Sikhs.

⇒💰 Economy & Industries

The Pakistani economy is traditionally driven by agriculture, with the country being a global leader in the production of cotton, wheat, rice, and sugarcane. Over the decades, the industrial sector has expanded significantly, anchored by a world-renowned textile manufacturing industry and sports goods production (such as hand-stitched soccer balls from Sialkot).
